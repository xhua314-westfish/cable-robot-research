## 1. Research Objective

### 1.1 Research Background

The research aims to develop a six-cable sensing system capable of estimating the six-degree-of-freedom (6-DOF) pose of a passively moving balloon-like object.

Previous investigations established:

- B1: Balloon geometry, engineering boundary and candidate anchor/attachment regions.
- B2: Initial six-cable configuration search and comparison.
- B3: Multi-pose geometric feasibility evaluation.
- B4: Measurement Jacobian, sensing sensitivity and predicted pose uncertainty.
- B5.1 Step 1.1–1.5: Investigation of Cable 5–6 interference and cable-pairing modifications.

Experiment B demonstrated favourable sensing performance but suffered cable–cable interference.

Modified B′ resolved the detected interference across the original 189 sampled poses by exchanging the attachments of Cable 5 and Cable 6. However, its sensing quality deteriorated significantly.

This motivates a more systematic configuration optimisation approach.

### 1.2 Research Question

**Why are the existing six-cable configurations insufficient, and what design requirements should guide further optimisation?**

### 1.3 Objectives

1. Audit the anchor and attachment distribution of Experiment B.
2. Verify the relationship between Experiment B and Modified B′.
3. Evaluate installation-point spacing and cable-direction similarity.
4. Define a Core Workspace for initial prototype development.
5. Establish consistent geometric and sensing-quality evaluation criteria.

### 2.1 Measurement Geometry

For Cable \(i\):

\[
B_i=P+Rr_i
\]

\[
D_i=A_i-B_i
\]

\[
L_i=\|D_i\|
\]

where:

- \(A_i\): World-frame anchor point.
- \(r_i\): Body-frame attachment point.
- \(P\): Balloon centre position.
- \(R\): Body-to-world rotation matrix.
- \(B_i\): World-frame attachment position.
- \(L_i\): Cable length.

A complete six-cable configuration is defined as:

\[
\mathcal C_6=\{(A_i,r_i)\}_{i=1}^{6}
\]

Anchor placement, attachment placement and cable pairing are separate design variables.

## 4. Lesson 1.2 — Configuration Distribution and Direction Audit

### Research Question

Are the anchors, attachments and cable directions sufficiently distributed?

### 4.1 Minimum Anchor Distance

The minimum anchor separation is:

\[
d_{A,\min}=\min_{i<j}\|A_i-A_j\|
\]

**MATLAB Result:**

\[
\boxed{d_{A,\min}=140.0000\ \mathrm{mm}}
\]

The closest anchor pairs include A3–A4 and A5–A6.

This does not indicate an immediate anchor-overlap problem.

However, minimum spacing alone does not represent the overall spatial distribution.

### 4.2 Minimum Attachment Distance

The minimum attachment separation is:

\[
d_{r,\min}=\min_{i<j}\|r_i-r_j\|
\]

**MATLAB Result:**

\[
\boxed{d_{r,\min}=35.9399\ \mathrm{mm}}
\]

This value represents the straight-line distance between two attachment points, not the spherical surface arc length.

Physical mounting feasibility remains unknown because connector dimensions and required attachment spacing have not yet been established.

### 4.3 Cable Direction Similarity

For each Cable:

\[
u_i=\frac{A_i-(P+Rr_i)}{\|A_i-(P+Rr_i)\|}
\]

Define the absolute direction similarity:

\[
S_{ij}=|u_i^Tu_j|
\]

Interpretation:

- \(S_{ij}\approx1\): Cable directions are nearly parallel or antiparallel.
- \(S_{ij}\approx0\): Cable directions are approximately orthogonal.

**MATLAB Result at the reference pose:**

\[
\boxed{S_{\max}=0.824441}
\]

Most similar pair:

**Cable 2 and Cable 6**

Corresponding acute angle between their unoriented directions:

\[
\alpha=\cos^{-1}(0.824441)\approx34.5^\circ
\]

Other relatively similar pairs included Cable 1–5 and Cable 3–4.

### 4.4 Interpretation

Cable-direction similarity describes only the three-dimensional translational measurement directions.

It does not include rotational sensitivity.

The full measurement Jacobian row is:

\[
H_i=
\begin{bmatrix}
-u_i^T&(u_i\times s_i)^T
\end{bmatrix}
\]

where:

\[
s_i=Rr_i
\]

Therefore, a high cable-direction similarity does not necessarily imply redundant six-degree-of-freedom measurements.

### Research Finding

Anchor distance, attachment distance and direction similarity provide useful geometric-distribution diagnostics.

However, they are insufficient as standalone sensing-quality indicators.

**Conclusion 2:** Include point spacing and direction similarity as auxiliary configuration metrics, while retaining the full Jacobian as the primary sensing evaluation method.

## 5. Lesson 1.3 — Core Workspace Definition

### Research Question

What initial motion range should the six-cable sensing prototype support?

### 5.1 Motivation

The original B3–B4 benchmark used 189 sampled poses spanning ±50 mm translation and ±10° single-axis rotations.

Requiring excellent performance over this entire range may be unnecessarily restrictive for the first adjustable experimental prototype.

Therefore, two evaluation regions are defined:

1. Core Workspace: Initial prototype performance requirement.
2. Benchmark Workspace: Wider-range configuration comparison.

These are engineering sampling regions rather than experimentally measured gastric-balloon motion boundaries.

### 5.2 Core Workspace

Translation samples:

\[
x,y,z\in\{-20,0,20\}\ \mathrm{mm}
\]

Orientation samples (Roll, Pitch, Yaw):

\[
\Theta_{\mathrm{core}}=
\left\{
\begin{array}{c}
(0,0,0)\\
(\pm5^\circ,0,0)\\
(0,\pm5^\circ,0)\\
(0,0,\pm5^\circ)
\end{array}
\right\}
\]

The total number of poses is:

\[
N_{\mathrm{core}}=3^3\times7=189
\]

The orientation set includes single-axis rotations only.

### 5.3 Benchmark Workspace

The original benchmark remains unchanged:

\[
x,y,z\in\{-50,0,50\}\ \mathrm{mm}
\]

with zero rotation and individual ±10° rotations about the three axes.

Total:

\[
N_{\mathrm{benchmark}}=189
\]

### 5.5 Limitations

The current verification confirms only that the rigid spherical balloon fits inside the rectangular engineering boundary at all 27 sampled centre positions.

It does not establish:

- Cable–sphere non-interference.
- Cable–cable clearance.
- Jacobian rank across all Core poses.
- Acceptable sensing uncertainty.
- Continuous workspace coverage.
- Positive-tension feasibility.

Combined multi-axis rotations and independent intermediate pose samples remain to be evaluated.

### Research Finding

A reproducible initial Core Workspace has been established with 189 sampled poses.

The chosen ±20 mm and ±5° ranges are provisional engineering assumptions and should be reviewed against experimental requirements.

**Conclusion 3:** Future configuration candidates must be evaluated on the same Core and Benchmark pose sets to ensure fair comparisons.

## 6. Lesson 1.4 — Configuration Acceptance Criteria

### Research Question

Which criteria should determine whether a six-cable configuration is suitable for further optimisation and prototype development?

### 6.1 Jacobian-Based Sensing Evaluation

The local measurement model is:

\[
\delta L\approx H\delta q
\]

For six cables and six rigid-body degrees of freedom:

\[
H\in\mathbb R^{6\times6}
\]

Use the same characteristic-length scaling as B4:

\[
H_s=H\operatorname{diag}
(1,1,1,1/r_b,1/r_b,1/r_b)
\]

The key sensing metrics are:

**Jacobian rank**

\[
\operatorname{rank}(H_s)=6
\]

This is required for local first-order rigid-pose identifiability.

**Minimum singular value**

\[
\sigma_{\min}(H_s)
\]

A larger value indicates stronger sensitivity along the weakest scaled pose direction.

**Condition number**

\[
\kappa(H_s)=
\frac{\sigma_{\max}(H_s)}{\sigma_{\min}(H_s)}
\]

A lower condition number generally indicates a better-conditioned local measurement mapping.

### 6.2 Reference-Pose Comparison

MATLAB results:

| Metric | Original B | Modified B′ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Minimum anchor distance (mm) | 140.0000 | 140.0000 |
| Minimum attachment distance (mm) | 35.9399 | 35.9399 |
| Maximum direction similarity | 0.824441 | 0.875150 |
| Jacobian rank | 6 | 6 |
| Scaled minimum singular value | 0.533600 | 0.075854 |
| Scaled condition number | 3.2365 | 23.575 |

These measurements correspond to:

\[
P=[0,0,0],\qquad R=I
\]

### 6.3 Interpretation

Original B and Modified B′ have identical anchor and attachment minimum distances.

However, changing their pairing alters:

- Cable direction vectors.
- Rotational sensitivity.
- Complete Jacobian row structure.
- Singular values.
- Condition number.

After the pairing modification:

- The minimum singular value decreased by approximately 85.8%.
- The condition number increased by approximately 7.3 times.

Both configurations remain full rank at the reference pose.

Therefore:

**Full rank is necessary for local identifiability but does not guarantee good sensing quality.**

### 6.4 Previously Established Benchmark Comparison

The earlier B5.1 analysis found:

| Indicator | Original B | Modified B′ |
|---|---:|---:|
| Geometrically passing sampled poses | 105/189 | 189/189 |
| Worst scaled condition number | 11.3888 | approximately 67,070 |

These figures come from the original wider Benchmark Workspace, not the newly defined Core Workspace.

They should not be confused with the reference-pose condition numbers reported in Section 6.2.

This comparison establishes an important design trade-off:

**Improving geometric feasibility through cable pairing can significantly reduce sensing robustness.**

### 6.5 Proposed Evaluation Framework

| Metric | Classification | Evaluation Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Balloon containment | Hard constraint | All required Core poses |
| Cable–sphere interference | Hard constraint | No unacceptable penetration |
| Cable–cable clearance | Hard constraint | Satisfy specified physical clearance |
| Minimum anchor distance | Mounting constraint / auxiliary metric | Meet hardware spacing requirements |
| Minimum attachment distance | Mounting constraint / auxiliary metric | Meet attachment-interface requirements |
| Direction similarity | Auxiliary metric | Diagnose direction clustering |
| Jacobian rank | Hard sensing constraint | Rank 6 at required Core poses |
| Minimum scaled singular value | Primary sensing objective | Maximise worst-case Core value |
| Scaled condition number | Sensing diagnostic | Avoid poorly conditioned poses |
| Benchmark pose coverage | Workspace comparison metric | Maximise feasible coverage |
| Cable length and stroke | Engineering constraint | Meet practical actuator and sensing limits |

Physical clearance thresholds, mounting distances and acceptable estimation-accuracy limits have not yet been determined experimentally.

No arbitrary numerical acceptance threshold is imposed at this stage.

### 6.6 Evaluation Priority

**Priority 1 — Geometric Feasibility**

Eliminate configurations with unacceptable balloon penetration or cable interference in the required Core poses.

**Priority 2 — Sensing Quality**

Among geometrically feasible candidates, prioritise strong worst-case scaled minimum singular values and reasonable condition numbers.

**Priority 3 — Distribution and Implementation**

Compare anchor and attachment spacing, cable-direction diversity, mounting accessibility and future prototype adjustability.

### Research Finding

Good geometric distribution does not necessarily imply good sensing performance.

**Conclusion 4:** Future optimisation must evaluate cable geometry and sensing quality jointly rather than treating spatial uniformity as the primary design objective.

## 7. Overall Research Conclusions

The November 9 configuration audit established five findings.

### Finding 1 — Existing Configuration B Is Not a Final Prototype

Experiment B has relatively favourable sensing quality, but previously detected cable–cable interference prevents direct adoption as the final configuration.

### Finding 2 — Pairing Has a Significant Effect on Sensing

Modified B′ preserves the original installation points but changes the measurement Jacobian and substantially reduces sensing robustness.

### Finding 3 — Spatial Distribution Is an Auxiliary Design Consideration

Anchor spacing, attachment spacing and cable-direction similarity are valuable diagnostics and potential engineering constraints.

However, they cannot replace full six-degree-of-freedom Jacobian evaluation.

### Finding 4 — Core Workspace Has Been Defined

The initial Core Workspace contains 189 sampled poses with:

- Translation: -20, 0, +20 mm on each axis.
- Rotation: identity and individual ±5° roll, pitch and yaw.

All sampled balloon-centre positions satisfy the rigid-sphere containment check.

### Finding 5 — Further Optimisation Requires Expanded Design Variables

The next configuration search should consider:

\[
\boxed{\mathcal C_6=\{(A_i,r_i)\}_{i=1}^{6}}
\]

with anchor placement, attachment placement and pairing treated as explicit design variables.

The objective is to identify practically implementable configurations satisfying geometric constraints while retaining adequate 6-DOF sensing performance.

## 8. Limitations and Unresolved Questions

The following questions remain open:

1. Can new anchor distributions eliminate cable interference without sacrificing sensing quality?
2. Does expanding the original 26 anchor candidates reveal better configurations?
3. Does a more uniform attachment candidate distribution improve configuration diversity?
4. What physical minimum distances are required for real anchors, attachments and cable routing?
5. How do sensing quality and geometric clearance vary across the newly defined Core Workspace?
6. Can promising configurations maintain performance under independent validation poses with simultaneous rotations?
7. Will low positive cable tension disturb passive balloon motion?
8. Can the final configuration remain usable when the rigid sphere is replaced by a flexible balloon?

The current audit does not establish continuous workspace feasibility, mechanical feasibility or experimentally measured pose accuracy.

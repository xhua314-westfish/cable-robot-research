**Application Scenario: Quantitative State Monitoring of a Passively Deformable Intragastric Balloon**

Intragastric balloons can undergo clinically significant changes, including deflation, hyperinflation and migration. 
Current clinical management includes scheduled follow-up and symptom assessment, 
with imaging or endoscopy used according to the clinical situation. 
However, a standardized and validated schedule for routine imaging-based monitoring of balloon geometry and position has not been established.

This motivates the investigation of a cable-robot-based measurement and sensing system for a passively moving and deformable balloon-like object in a stomach-like constrained environment. 
The system aims to investigate whether cable length, displacement and/or tension measurements can be used to estimate the object's position,
orientation and deformation/shape over time, without assuming that its motion trajectory is known in advance.

The initial research objective is to establish the measurement model, assess state observability and estimation accuracy, 
and validate shape and state reconstruction experimentally. 
Its potential clinical value would be to provide additional quantitative information between conventional clinical assessments. 
Whether this information can enable earlier detection of clinically significant changes remains to be established.
